3. RQ1 violin analyses (LMM)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A: en - mean(non-English) | paper-primary (model x language intercepts; Satterthwaite t): +1.295 [1.156, 1.435] p < 0.001
  B: focus - other non-English languages (is_focus) | paper-primary (model x language intercepts; Satterthwaite t; singular fit): +0.406 [0.298, 0.513] p < 0.001
  ATTENTION: English sentence pairs shared by several origin languages: 6123 model x English sentence pairs occur under >= 2 origin languages (14586 of 64740 English rows); 176 of them have different saved ΔSLOR

Results (paper):
  English-centric models assign a higher mean ΔSLOR to native strategies in English than in the six other languages (difference 1.30, 95% CI [1.16, 1.43], Satterthwaite t (df = 227.28); 28 models, 121,212 observations). The per-model difference is positive for 28 of 28 models (descriptive).
  Non-English-centric models show a higher mean ΔSLOR in their focus language than in other non-English languages, controlling for language (β = 0.41, 95% CI [0.30, 0.51], Satterthwaite t (df = 59.09); 11 models). With 11 models this generalises only cautiously across model families; no language-adjusted focus advantage for Bode 13B.

Caveats
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Significance vs effect size: with thousands of sentence pairs, very small effects become significant; read the estimates and CIs, not only p.
- Mean vs distribution: all tests concern mean ΔSLOR; equal means do not imply equal distributions and non-significant differences are not evidence of equality.
- Correlation vs causation: regression coefficients and Spearman correlations are associations.
- English rows: 6123 model x English sentence pairs occur under several origin languages in the failure-rate selection (counted once per origin there); 176 of them have different saved scores for identical sentences.
